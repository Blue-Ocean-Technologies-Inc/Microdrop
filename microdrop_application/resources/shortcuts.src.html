Keyboard shortcuts, gamepad and Touch Assist

Every key MicroDrop answers to, in one place, and the on-screen tools for a bench with only a touch panel: the Virtual Numpad, Virtual Keyboard, Virtual D-pad and Virtual Mouse under Tools ▸ Touch Assist. It also says how to add a game controller.

Start with the walkthrough. The shortcut finder below it lists every key and can be filtered by pane or by pressing the key itself. The two clips are real recordings and loop while they are on screen.

Overview

A key goes to the pane you clicked last. The same letter can mean different things in different panes: D switches the Device Viewer to Draw mode, but jumps to the last step in the protocol pane.

Quick walkthrough

Five short tries, from the key list to moving a droplet with your finger. It assumes a chip is loaded in the Device Viewer.

  1. 1Open the key list

    Click inside the Device Viewer, then press F1 (or Ctrl+/). A Keyboard Shortcuts window opens with every Device Viewer key, in six sections: Selection, Routes, Modes, View, Actions and Help. Right-click the chip ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts… opens the same window. It stays open while you work.

  2. 2Move a droplet with the arrow keys

    Switch on the electrodes under a droplet (click them), then press →. Every switched-on electrode moves one place to the right and the droplet follows. Shift+arrow grows the switched-on area, Ctrl+arrow shrinks it, and Ctrl+Shift+arrow pulls it apart to split the droplet. Try them in the demo first.

  3. 3Step through a protocol

    Click a step in the Pluggable Protocol Tree. Press S for the next step and W for the previous one; the Device Viewer shows each step's electrodes. A and D jump to the first and last step.

  4. 4On a touch panel: the on-screen D-pad

    Open Tools ▸ Touch Assist ▸ Virtual D-pad. A small D-pad window appears at the bottom right of the screen; drag it by its title to where you want it. Tap the Device Viewer, then tap the arrows: they act exactly like the arrow keys. Tap ⇧, ctrl or alt to hold that key down until you tap it again.

  5. 5On a touch panel: type a number

    Open Tools ▸ Touch Assist ▸ Virtual Numpad. Tap the box you want to change (a voltage, a duration), then tap the digits and ⏎. ▲ and ▼ step a number box up and down. The numpad demo shows why you tap the box first.

Shortcut finder

All shortcuts

The Device Viewer, Image Viewer and quick-action rows are the same lists MicroDrop itself uses, word for word.

Device Viewer keys

Click inside the device view first. Most of these keys change electrodes or routes, so they work in the editing modes (Mode: Edit, Draw, Merge) and not while a protocol runs.

Arrow keys

Move every switched-on electrode one place in the arrow's direction. The droplet on them follows.

A step of the whole switched-on area. An electrode with no neighbour that way stays where it is.

To walk a droplet across the chip without clicking each electrode. With nothing switched on, the arrows start from the last electrode you moved to.

The arrow always moves the way it points on screen, even after you rotate the device view with R. A held key keeps stepping.

One electrode per press. Works in Edit, Draw and Merge modes.

Shift / Ctrl + arrow: grow and shrink

Shift+arrow adds a row of electrodes on that side; Ctrl+arrow takes the row on that side away.

Grow the selection switches on the neighbours of the electrodes furthest in that direction. Shrink the selection from a side switches off the electrodes furthest in that direction.

Grow to stretch a droplet onto more electrodes (to pull it or to make room for a bigger one); shrink to let go of one side.

Shrinking the last electrode switches everything off.

One row per press.

Ctrl + Shift + arrow: split

Pulls the switched-on area apart in two halves, to split a droplet.

The first press picks the direction: ← / → split side to side, ↑ / ↓ top to bottom. On a view you have not rotated, → or ↓ pulls the two halves one step further apart; after R the directions turn with the chip. The first press only opens the gap: it switches off the middle electrode of an odd number, moves one half of an even number, or splits a single electrode onto its two neighbours. The opposite arrow takes one step back, and all the way back makes the droplet whole again.

To split one droplet into two. Several separate droplets switched on at once each split on their own.

Letting go of Ctrl or Shift ends the split: the next press starts a new one from whatever is switched on then.

Three or four electrodes in a line is a good start.

Alt + arrow, Backspace, Del: routes

Draw a route one electrode at a time from the keyboard, take back its last electrode, or delete it.

Alt+arrow adds the next electrode to the route; with no route selected it starts a new one, in Draw mode, from the last electrode you moved to (or the one switched-on electrode). Backspace erases the selected route's last electrode; on a two-electrode route it deletes the route. Del deletes the selected route.

For a route that must follow exact electrodes, or on a bench where a mouse drag is awkward.

Works in Draw and Edit modes only, so it never throws away a merge, a channel edit or a zone you are drawing. Esc stops drawing.

D, E, A, N, Space, Esc: modes

Single letters switch the Device Viewer's mode; the label under the mode buttons shows it (Mode: Draw).

D Draw, E Edit, A Auto (Autoroute), N Channel Edit, Space Pan (drag the view). Pressing the same key again goes back to the mode you were in before. Esc leaves Pan mode or route drawing, and cancels a zone you are drawing.

To switch tools without reaching for the mode buttons.

In Channel Edit mode, while you type a channel number, Return keeps it and Esc cancels it; the other keys go to the number, not to the shortcuts.

The Device Viewer starts in Draw mode.

Zoom, fit and rotate

Change how the chip is shown, never what is switched on. These work during a run too.

Ctrl++ (or Ctrl+=) zooms in, Ctrl+- zooms out, Ctrl+mouse wheel does both, Ctrl+0 fits the whole device. On a touch panel, pinch with two fingers. R rotates the device view; Shift+R rotates the camera picture.

Zoom in on a small droplet; rotate so the chip on screen matches the chip on the bench.

Each rotation is a quarter turn. The arrow keys follow the rotation, so ↑ is always up on screen.

90° per press.

X, Shift+X, F, T: actions

Clear, find liquid, and switch realtime on or off.

X switches every electrode off. Shift+X clears every route. F switches every electrode off, then marks where MicroDrop detects liquid (the same as right-click ▸ Find Liquid). T turns realtime mode on or off.

F after loading droplets, to see where they are. T to stop the DropBot from following your clicks while you set up.

T needs a connected DropBot; without one the key does nothing. It is the one action key that also works during a run. F is ignored during a run.

Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y: undo and redo

Take back, or put back, the last change in the Device Viewer.

Ctrl+Z undoes; Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes. Same as the Undo and Redo buttons beside the mode buttons.

The protocol pane has its own undo with the same keys: whichever pane you clicked last is the one that undoes.

F1: the key list

Opens the Keyboard Shortcuts window.

A window listing every Device Viewer key, grouped as Selection, Routes, Modes, View, Actions and Help. Ctrl+/ and right-click ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts… open it too.

Whenever you forget a key. It stays open beside the window; pressing F1 again brings it to the front instead of opening another.

In the Image Viewer, F1 opens that viewer's own list, Image Viewer Keyboard Shortcuts; Help ▸ Image Viewer Keyboard Shortcuts… opens it from the menu.

Protocol pane keys

Click anywhere in the Pluggable Protocol Tree pane first. The navigation keys follow the buttons: a key does nothing when its button is greyed out or hidden.

W, S, A, D: step through

Move between steps without the mouse.

W Previous Step, S Next Step, A First Step, D Last Step. Ctrl+← / Ctrl+→ are Previous Phase / Next Phase, shown when a run is paused in phase-by-phase mode. Ctrl+. is Stop Protocol.

To check each step's electrodes in the Device Viewer while you write a protocol, or to step a paused run.

Next Step on the last step adds a new step, as the button does.

Ctrl+R: play, pause, resume

Clicks whichever of Play, Pause or Resume is showing.

Idle, it plays the protocol; running, it pauses; paused, it resumes. The step list's right-click entry Run Selected Steps is also bound to Ctrl+R.

To start or pause a run while your hand is on the keyboard.

Today, Ctrl+R does nothing while the step list has the keyboard, which is right after you click a step: the two jobs clash and neither runs. Click a navigation button first (or use the Play button, and right-click ▸ Run Selected Steps).

Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y: undo protocol edits

Take back or put back the last change to the protocol.

Ctrl+Z undoes; Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes. The Undo and Redo buttons under the step list do the same and name the edit in their tooltip.

After deleting the wrong rows or typing a wrong value.

Ignored while a protocol runs. While you are typing in a cell, the keys undo your typing in that cell instead.

Remembers the last 100 edits.

Quick-action keys

The buttons under the step list, from the keyboard.

Ctrl+Return Add step below selection, Ctrl+Shift+Return Add group, Ctrl+Backspace Delete last step, Ctrl+Shift+I Import protocol into selected group, Ctrl+O Open, Ctrl+S Save, Ctrl+N New protocol, R Browse session reports.

A key does nothing while its button is greyed out, and none of them work during a run.

Step list keys

Keys that work after clicking a step.

Ctrl+C / X / V copy, cut and paste rows. Ctrl+G Fold into Group, Ctrl+Shift+G Unfold Group, Ctrl+Shift+F Fill Pattern…, Delete deletes the selected rows (no question asked; undo brings them back). Esc deselects the step, so the Device Viewer goes back to free mode.

Grouping, Fill Pattern and Run Selected Steps are locked during a run, like their menu entries. The Protocol Tree tutorial explains each of them.

Other windows

Smaller sets of keys in the Image Viewer, the Edit Connections dialog, the note window and the menus.

Image Viewer

Draw and edit regions of interest, page through images, and zoom.

The full list is in the finder (pick Image Viewer) and in the viewer's own key list: press F1 with the image clicked, or Help ▸ Image Viewer Keyboard Shortcuts…. While you trace a polygon, Return closes it, Backspace takes back the last point and Esc throws it away; a second Esc puts the tool away.

In the perspective window, Ctrl+mouse wheel zooms and Space turns drag-panning on and off. The Image Analysis tutorial covers the tools themselves.

Edit Connections

The dialog for adding and removing the lines that say which electrodes are neighbours.

Click a line to select it (Ctrl-click for several, Shift-drag for a box), then Delete or Backspace removes it. Ctrl+Z undoes; Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y redoes. Scroll to zoom.

Note window

The note opened with New Note in the protocol pane.

Ctrl+S Save Note, Ctrl+B Bold, Ctrl+I Italic, Ctrl+U Underline, Ctrl+Shift+S Strikethrough, Ctrl+Shift+L Bullet List.

Click in the note first: in the protocol pane Ctrl+S saves the protocol, and in the main window Ctrl+Shift+S opens Sync Remote Experiments.

Menu keys

Keys shown beside a menu entry work anywhere in the main window.

Ctrl+Shift+S opens Tools ▸ Remote Controls ▸ Sync Remote Experiments.... A layout preset can have a key of your choice: give it one when you save it (View ▸ Layout Presets ▸ Save Current Layout...) or later in Manage Layout Presets....

When you pick a preset key that is already taken (by a menu, or by a Device Viewer key) the dialog warns you. A menu key wins over a Device Viewer key, so avoid single letters and the arrows.

Gamepad

A game controller can drive the Device Viewer like the arrow keys. It is a separate plugin, not part of MicroDrop itself.

Gamepad plugin

Adds a game controller to the Device Viewer: its D-pad steps the switched-on electrodes, and its buttons clear, find liquid and so on.

The gamepad-microdrop-plugin package. It moves the same switched-on electrodes as the arrow keys and the Virtual D-pad, so you can mix all three.

For long sessions of moving droplets by hand, or on a bench without a keyboard.

  1. Open Tools ▸ Manage Plugins… and click Install Plugin…. The Browse Plugins window lists the plugin channel.
  2. Select gamepad-microdrop-plugin and click Install.
  3. Back in Manage Plugins, tick its group under Available Groups and click Apply.

Switching the group on is what turns the gamepad on. The button map and timings are in the plugin's own preferences tab, and its icon shows the controller state.

Off: not installed with MicroDrop. Gamepad settings saved by an older MicroDrop are moved to the plugin the first time the Device Viewer starts.

Touch Assist

Floating helpers for a bench with a touch panel and no mouse or keyboard. Each is a tick box in Tools ▸ Touch Assist; tick it to show the tool, untick it (or tap the tool's ✕) to hide it.

How the tools behave

They float above MicroDrop and never take the keyboard away from what you tapped.

A pad sends its keys to the box or pane you tapped last, exactly as a real key would. Tapping a pad key does not move that focus, so the order is always: tap the target, then tap the keys.

Each tool first appears near the bottom of the screen. Drag a pad by its title strip; drag the mouse by its body. They work inside MicroDrop only.

Pad keys are at least 44 px across, big enough for a fingertip.

Virtual Numpad

Digits, minus, decimal point, ⌫, ▲, ▼ and ⏎ for typing numbers.

A 4 × 4 pad titled Numpad. ⌫ deletes, ⏎ is Enter, ▲ and ▼ step a number box up or down.

To type a voltage, a duration or any value in a protocol cell or settings box.

Hold ▲ or ▼ to keep stepping.

▲ / ▼ start repeating after 0.4 s, then every 0.08 s.

Virtual Keyboard

A small QWERTY keyboard for names and notes.

A digit row, three letter rows, ⇧, ⌫, ⏎, ctrl, alt, space, . and _.

To name a step or a protocol, or type a note.

⇧, ctrl and alt are one-shot, like a phone keyboard: tap ⇧ then a for A (the letters turn capital while ⇧ is on); tap ctrl then c for Ctrl+C. Then they let go by themselves. With ⇧ the digit row types !@#$%^&*().

Virtual D-pad

The Device Viewer's arrow keys on screen, with sticky modifiers.

Four arrows, ⇧, ctrl, alt, Esc and ⌫. Every Device Viewer arrow key works from it: arrows alone step, ⇧ grows, ctrl shrinks, ⇧ + ctrl split, alt draws a route; Esc stops route drawing and ⌫ erases the route's last electrode.

To move or split droplets on a touch panel. Tap the Device Viewer once first so it has the keyboard.

Unlike the Virtual Keyboard, ⇧, ctrl and alt stay down (lit) until you tap them again, so you can split with several taps. Tapping ⇧ or ctrl off ends the split, like letting go of the real keys. Hiding the D-pad lets go of all three.

A held arrow repeats after 0.4 s, then every 0.08 s.

Virtual Mouse

A mouse-shaped pad with a crosshair above it, for exact clicks where a finger is too big.

Drag the body to aim: the crosshair rides above it, so your finger never hides the target. Tap L or R to left- or right-click at the crosshair; two quick taps are a double-click. Drag the strip between L and R up or down to scroll. hold keeps the left button pressed: drag the body to drag whatever is under the crosshair, then tap L or hold to let go.

Right-click menus, small buttons, dragging a route point or a dock edge.

The ✕ in its corner hides it. A tap that moves more than a few pixels counts as a drag, not a click.

One scroll notch per 12 px dragged on the strip.

Caveats

  • A key goes to the pane you clicked last. D, A, R, Ctrl+Z and others mean different things in the Device Viewer and the protocol pane. If a key does something unexpected, click the pane you meant and press it again.
  • Ctrl+R does nothing in the step list. Play and Run Selected Steps share it and clash there. Click a navigation button first, or use the Play button and the right-click menu.
  • Single-letter protocol keys go to a cell you are typing in. While a cell is open for typing, letters type into it, and Ctrl+Z undoes the typing, not the protocol.
  • Ctrl+Shift+S is not "save as". In the main window it opens Sync Remote Experiments; in a note window it is Strikethrough.
  • During a run the Device Viewer only accepts view keys and T, and the protocol quick-action keys are off.
  • The Touch Assist pads type into what you tapped last. Tap the box or the Device Viewer first; with nothing tapped, a pad key does nothing.
  • The gamepad needs its plugin. MicroDrop alone has no gamepad support.

Glossary

Focus
Which pane gets the keys: the one you clicked or tapped last.
Switched-on electrodes
The electrodes with voltage on them (coloured in the Device Viewer); the arrow keys move them.
Route
A path drawn in the Device Viewer that a droplet is walked along.
Display mode
The Device Viewer's mode while a protocol runs: it shows the step and ignores editing keys.
Free mode
No step selected: the Device Viewer shows what you switch on by hand.
Latch
A modifier key on a Touch Assist pad that stays down after you tap it.
Modifier
Shift, Ctrl or Alt: held with another key to change what it does.
Quick action
One of the buttons under the step list: Add step, Save, Open and the rest.
