Dropbot Status & Controls

The Dropbot Status And Controls pane shows whether MicroDrop can talk to your DropBot and whether a chip is in it. It switches the high voltage on and off (Realtime), sets the voltage and frequency, and shows what the DropBot measures back: capacitance, force and an estimate of droplet volume.

This tutorial also covers the dialogs the DropBot can raise (halt, shorts, no power), the Tools ▸ Dropbot menu with its self-tests, and the DropBot preferences. Each card says what it is, when you would use it, how to read it and the value MicroDrop starts with. The two clips are real recordings; the interactive figures let you try the pane without a DropBot.

Overview: where things are, and when high voltage is on

Four places in MicroDrop belong to the DropBot.

  • The pane, docked on the right above the protocol tree. Left: a picture of the DropBot, Connection, Chip Status and the Realtime button. Right: the readings, in three columns: name, what the DropBot reports, and the box you set.
  • The status bar at the bottom of the window: a droplet icon whose colour is the connection state, and a realtime switch .
  • Tools ▸ Dropbot: self-tests, Search for Dropbot Connection and Reboot Dropbot.
  • Edit ▸ Preferences: the Dropbot Settings and DropBot Status & Controls pages, plus a few DropBot settings on other pages (see Preferences).

When is high voltage on? Clicking an electrode only lights it in the Device Viewer. The DropBot puts high voltage on the lit electrodes only in these cases:

  • Realtime On. The high-voltage output is switched on. Every electrode lit in the Device Viewer gets the voltage at once, including the ones that were already lit before you switched Realtime on.
  • A protocol run (not a preview run). The run switches Realtime on by itself before the first step.
  • Find Liquid. It switches the high-voltage output on at 1 kHz for the measurement, even with Realtime off, then puts the DropBot back as it was.
  • Three self-tests: Test high voltage, On-board feedback calibration and Scan test board (see Self-tests).

High voltage is off with Realtime off, after a halt, and while the DropBot is disconnected.

Quick walkthrough

From plugging in the DropBot to moving droplets with a known force. The Buttons and icons section shows every icon as it looks in the app.

  1. 1Connect

    Plug in the DropBot's power supply and USB cable. MicroDrop looks for it every 2 seconds on its own. Connection turns to Active. The picture's background and the status-bar droplet turn orange, then green once the DropBot reports a chip (Chip Status: Present).

    On every connection MicroDrop switches the high voltage off, turns all electrodes off and checks the chip for shorted channels. You then see Shorts Detected or No Shorts Detected (see Dialogs). Not found after a while? Use Tools ▸ Dropbot ▸ Search for Dropbot Connection.

  2. 2Set voltage and frequency first

    Type or scroll in the boxes on the right. With Realtime off nothing is sent yet: MicroDrop keeps the latest value and sends it when you switch Realtime on. The middle column shows "-" until then.

    The boxes accept only the range set in the preferences (30 to 140 V and 100 to 10 000 Hz at first). See Voltage.

  3. 3Switch Realtime on: high voltage is now on

    Click Realtime Off so it turns green and reads Realtime On (or click in the status bar, or press T over the Device Viewer). The DropBot switches its high-voltage output on and applies your voltage and frequency. Every electrode that is lit in the Device Viewer gets the voltage straight away. Clear the electrodes first if you do not want that.

    The middle column fills in: the voltage the DropBot measures, the frequency, and the capacitance of the lit electrodes.

  4. 4Calibrate, then read force and volume

    In the Device Viewer's calibration buttons: light a few electrodes with only filler (air or oil) over them and click . Then light electrodes fully under liquid and click . The pane now shows Force and the Volume estimate.

    Set the plate gap of your chip in the preferences so the volume is right, and pick your dielectric material to see its thickness.

  5. 5Change the voltage while you work

    With Realtime on, a new value goes to the DropBot a third of a second after you stop typing or scrolling. The measured voltage and the force follow. The measured value can sit a volt or so off the box.

  6. 6Switch off, or deal with trouble

    Click Realtime On again to switch the high voltage off when you are done.

    If a DropBot Halted, Shorts Detected or ERROR: No Power window appears, look it up in Which window, and what to do. If readings look wrong, run the self-tests in Tools ▸ Dropbot and read the Test Results.

The force, volume and dielectric readings need a calibration from the Device Viewer.

Find Liquid (droplet detection)

Asks the DropBot which electrodes have liquid on them and lights those electrodes in the Device Viewer.

The DropBot switches its high-voltage output on at 1 kHz, measures the capacitance of each channel of the loaded device, and counts a channel as wet when it reads above the Drop Detect Capacitance. Then it puts the voltage, frequency and output back as they were. Pressing F also clears the lit electrodes first; the menu item does not.

To find where droplets are after loading a chip, or before running a step that needs to know.

The wet electrodes light up a moment later. High voltage is on during the measurement, even with Realtime off, and with Realtime on the newly lit electrodes stay powered.

Threshold 10 pF (Drop Detect Capacitance).

Status, settings and readings

Middle column: what the DropBot reports. Right column: what you ask for. A "-" means no reading.

Connection

Whether MicroDrop is talking to a DropBot.

Active once MicroDrop has opened the DropBot on its USB port; Inactive otherwise. MicroDrop looks for a DropBot every 2 seconds until it finds one, and starts looking again after a disconnection.

Check it first whenever nothing seems to reach the chip.

The status-bar droplet and the picture's background are grey while Inactive. Connecting also clears a halt.

Inactive at start-up, Active a few seconds after the DropBot is plugged in and powered.

Chip Status (chip insert detection)

Whether a chip is in the DropBot.

Present or Absent. The DropBot senses the chip through its output-enable contact and reports each change; MicroDrop also asks once when it connects, in case the chip went in before.

After inserting or swapping a chip, before switching Realtime on.

Present turns the picture to the "chip inserted" photo and the colour from orange to green. Disconnecting sets it back to Absent.

Absent until a connected DropBot reports a chip.

Realtime mode

The switch for the DropBot's high-voltage output.

On: the output is on; the lit electrodes get the voltage, and new voltage, frequency and electrode changes reach the DropBot as you make them. Switching on also sends the latest voltage, frequency and the set of lit electrodes. Off: the output is off. Voltage and frequency changes are kept and sent when you switch on; electrode clicks only change the picture.

On while you move droplets by hand. Off to set things up, to swap a chip, or whenever nothing should be driven.

Green Realtime On button and green . While off, Voltage, Frequency, Capacitance, Force and Dielectric read "-".

Off. The pane's button works only while connected, not halted, and while no protocol runs. A protocol run switches it on before the first step. Afterwards, if it was off, the run switches it off again; if it was on, MicroDrop asks Keep Realtime Mode Enabled Post-Protocol? (or follows your saved answer). Any halt switches it off.

Free mode

Working on the chip by hand, with no protocol step selected.

MicroDrop is in free mode while no step (or only a group) is selected in the protocol tree. Selecting a step shows that step on the Device Viewer instead.

Set voltage and frequency in free mode: the pane's boxes are locked while a step is selected, because the step's own voltage and frequency apply.

The status bar says Free Mode, Editing: Step N or Running Protocol: Step N.

Free mode when nothing is selected.

Halted

The DropBot stopped driving the chip by itself after a fault.

Two faults halt the DropBot: output current exceeded and chip load saturated. Either one switches Realtime and the high voltage off and shows DropBot Halted. A short does not halt the DropBot; it is reported separately (Shorts Detected).

Not something you switch: read the window, then fix the cause.

After output current exceeded the colour turns red and the pane's Realtime button and voltage and frequency boxes stay locked until the DropBot reconnects. After chip load saturated the colour does not change and you can switch Realtime on again.

Not halted.

Voltage

How hard the DropBot drives the lit electrodes: more voltage, more force on the droplet.

The box is the voltage you ask for, in whole volts. The middle value is the voltage the DropBot measures on its output; it updates when it moves by more than 1 V.

Raise it when droplets move sluggishly or not at all; lower it if droplets split, stick, or the dielectric breaks down.

The box accepts only Min Voltage to Max Voltage from Edit ▸ Preferences ▸ Protocol Settings ▸ Protocol Setters Config. The DropBot has its own limits: at least 30 V, and at most the Max Voltage it reports (see Hardware Config). A value outside those is refused by the DropBot; the pane does not warn you, but the measured value does not follow. Edits go out 0.3 s after you stop changing the box. Locked while a protocol runs, while a step is selected and after an over-current halt.

100 V, within 30 to 140 V. MicroDrop remembers the last value you set and starts with it.

Frequency

How fast the drive voltage alternates, in hertz (cycles per second).

The box sets it in steps of 100 Hz. The middle value repeats the setting while Realtime is on; it is not measured.

Most work stays at the default; change it when your chip or method calls for another.

Shown with an SI prefix: 10 kHz is 10 000 Hz. The range, the DropBot's own limits (at least 100 Hz, at most its reported Max Frequency) and the locking work as for Voltage.

10 000 Hz (10 kHz), within 100 Hz to 10 kHz.

Capacitance

What the DropBot measures across the lit electrodes. Liquid over an electrode raises it a lot; filler raises it a little.

In picofarads (pF). The DropBot sends a reading every Capacitance Update Interval. The pane shows the average of 5 readings, and only when it moves by more than 3 pF, to keep it steady.

To see whether a droplet sits on the lit electrodes, and as the input to volume.

A jump when you light an electrode under a droplet means the droplet is there. "-" while Realtime is off.

Depends on the chip and on how much is lit. In the clips: about 3 pF with nothing lit and 100 to 170 pF with a droplet on a few lit electrodes.

Droplet readout: Volume, Fill and Covered area

An estimate of how much liquid sits on the lit electrodes, worked out from the capacitance and your calibration.

The Device Viewer splits the capacitance into a liquid part and a filler part. Covered area = (capacitance − Cf × lit area) ÷ (Cl − Cf), never below 0. Volume = covered area × plate gap. Fill = covered area ÷ lit area. Without a filler calibration, Cf counts as 0 and the volume reads high.

To check that a dispensed or split droplet has about the volume you expect.

Pick the readout with the drop-down; hover the value to see all three plus c_device. "-" with a reason: No volume estimate from the device viewer yet., Measure the liquid capacitance to calibrate., Liquid calibration must exceed the filler calibration., No actuated electrodes. or No capacitance reading. It updates when the volume moves by more than 0.1 µL. A fill above 100 % means liquid outside the lit electrodes or an old calibration. Unlike the other readings it is not cleared when Realtime goes off, so it can show an old value then.

Volume in µL with one decimal (4.4 µL in the first clip), Fill in %, Covered area in mm². The plate gap starts at 254 µm (Plate gap).

Droplet Readouts window

A small window listing Volume, Fill, Covered area and c_device together.

Opened by . It stays open next to the main window and updates live; the reason for a missing estimate is shown at its bottom.

When you want all droplet numbers in view while the pane shows only one, for example while dispensing.

Same values and "-" rules as the readout row.

Closed.

c_device

The chip's capacitance per square millimetre due to the liquid, from your calibration.

Cl − Cf, in pF/mm² (see Calibration buttons). It feeds Force and Dielectric.

Calibrate once per chip and liquid.

Not a row of its own: hover the droplet readout value, or open the Droplet Readouts window. "-" until both Cl and Cf are measured. It does not clear when Realtime goes off.

No single value: it depends on the dielectric's material and thickness, and thinner layers read higher. One recording: Cl 7.2 and Cf 1.3 pF/mm², so c_device 5.9 pF/mm².

Force

The pull on a droplet's edge per metre, from c_device and the measured voltage.

c_device × voltage² ÷ 2, in millinewtons per metre (mN/m). Doubling the voltage gives four times the force.

To compare runs on different chips or voltages, or to find a voltage that gives the force a protocol needs.

Uses the measured voltage (middle column), not the box. "-" without both calibrations or with Realtime off.

In the clips: 35.8 mN/m at 110 V and 58.9 mN/m at 141 V with c_device 5.9 pF/mm².

Dielectric (thickness)

The thickness of the insulating layer between the electrodes and the droplets, worked out from c_device.

Relative permittivity of the picked material × the permittivity of free space ÷ c_device, in µm.

To check a new batch of chips: a thinner or thicker coating than expected shows here.

"-" until a material is picked and both calibrations exist. It also clears when Realtime goes off; if it stays "-" after you switch Realtime back on, pick the material again.

Example: Parylene C with c_device 5.9 pF/mm² gives 4.65 µm. The row is shown by default.

Dialogs

Windows the DropBot can raise, with their exact titles. Pick the one you see.

Which window, and what to do

DropBot Halted

The DropBot cut the high voltage by itself.

Text: DropBot has halted because output current was exceeded. or DropBot has halted because chip load feedback exceeded allowable range., with advice below it. One OK button; no Retry.

Too much current (a short through liquid, a damaged chip) or too much load (too many droplets or electrodes at once).

Realtime is off when you see it. See Halted for what stays locked.

Over-current: check the chip, then unplug all DropBot cables and plug them back in, as the window advises. Chip load: light fewer electrodes, check for damage, then switch Realtime on again.

Shorts Detected and No Shorts Detected

The result of the DropBot's check for shorted channels.

Shorts Detected: Shorts detected on channels: [3, 17]. [RISKY] Would you like keep these channels enabled? with Yes and No. No Shorts Detected: No shorts were detected.

On every connection, after Detect shorted channels, and whenever the DropBot reports a short on its own (not during self-tests).

No disables the listed channels: the Device Viewer shows them as disabled and they are not driven. Yes keeps them enabled, which is risky. Re-enable a channel later from the Device Viewer's right-click menu (Enable Electrode / Enable All Electrodes). At a connection, No shorts has a Do not show again (can be undone from preferences) box; after a test you asked for it is always shown.

Answer No unless you know why the channel reads shorted.

ERROR: No Power

The DropBot answered on USB but has no power supply.

DropBot currently has no power supply connected. Plug in power supply cable. Click the "Retry" button after plugging in the power cable to attempt reconnection, with a picture of the power socket and a Retry button.

The power supply is unplugged or switched off while USB is connected.

While this window is up MicroDrop stops looking for the DropBot on its own.

Plug the power in, then click Retry: MicroDrop starts looking again and closes the window.

Tools ▸ Dropbot: self-tests, search and reboot

Run the self-tests when readings look wrong or after a halt. A self-test needs a connected DropBot; without one you get Dropbot Disconnected (DropBot is not connected. Plug in the DropBot USB cable and power supply.). While a test runs, the DropBot's normal shorts warnings are muted. Each test puts the voltage, frequency, output and electrodes back as they were when it ends.

Test high voltage

Checks that the DropBot reaches the voltages it is asked for.

Switches the high-voltage output on and steps through 5 voltages from the DropBot's lowest to its highest, measuring each. Then repeats with the output disconnected from the electrodes, for comparison. It waits about 3 seconds in total between steps.

When the measured voltage does not follow the box, or after an over-current halt.

Test voltage Results: a plot Measured vs Target Voltage. The dashed line is a perfect match; the dots are the measurements. Dots far below the line at high voltage mean the DropBot cannot reach them.

Risk: electrodes that are lit stay lit and get up to the maximum voltage. Clear the electrodes, or take the chip out, before you run it.

On-board feedback calibration

Checks the capacitance-measuring circuit against reference capacitors built into the DropBot.

Sets 100 V, switches the output on with every electrode off, and measures each built-in reference capacitor 10 times.

When capacitance readings look wrong on a chip you trust.

Test on board feedback calibration Results: a plot Measured vs Nominal Capacitance with error bars. Points on the dashed line are good.

No chip or test board needed. High voltage is on during the test.

Detect shorted channels

Asks the DropBot which channels are shorted.

The same check MicroDrop runs on every connection, run on demand. MicroDrop asks you to insert the test board first.

After a Shorts Detected window you doubt, or to check the DropBot without a chip.

A Shorts Detected window listing the channels, or No Shorts Detected. See Shorts for the Yes / No choice.

No high voltage. With the test board in, no shorts is the expected result.

Scan test board

Steps through every channel of the DropBot with the test board in.

Checks for shorts first, then sets 100 V and switches on one channel at a time, measuring its capacitance. Shorted channels are skipped.

To find a dead channel after a halt, or when one electrode never moves liquid.

Test channels Results: a bar per channel (red when under 5 pF) and a histogram, then Failed channels (N): … in red or All channels passed. in green. A channel fails when it reads under 5 pF. Skipped shorted channels read 0, so they are listed as failed too.

Needs the DropBot test board. High voltage is on, one channel at a time.

Run all on-board self-tests

Runs every check in one go and writes a report.

In order: system information, system metrics (temperature and reference voltage), a scan of the boards inside the DropBot, Test high voltage, shorts, On-board feedback calibration and Scan test board.

To send to support, or as a full check of a new or repaired DropBot.

No Test Results window: the report is saved as an HTML file in Documents/Sci-Bots/Microdrop and opened in your web browser, with links to the raw data behind each plot.

Needs the test board. High voltage is on during the voltage, calibration and scan parts; the same risk as Test high voltage applies.

Test Results

The window a single plotted self-test opens when it finishes.

A live plot with a toolbar (zoom, pan, save), a Raw data JSON link to the data file, and for Scan test board the failed-channel line. The data file is saved in Documents/Sci-Bots/Microdrop with the test's name and the time.

Read it before you close it; it does not come back.

Pick an outcome below to see what it means.

Opens a moment after the progress window says the test is complete.

Search for Dropbot Connection

Makes MicroDrop look for the DropBot again.

If a DropBot is already connected, MicroDrop re-announces the connection and checks the chip again. Otherwise it starts, or resumes, looking for the DropBot's USB device every 2 seconds.

When Connection stays Inactive after plugging in, for example after the search stopped for No Power.

Connection turns Active once found. It never switches the high voltage on.

Not needed in normal use: the search runs on its own.

Reboot Dropbot

Restarts the DropBot's microcontroller.

Asks first, in Dropbot Reboot Warning: Rebooting the DropBot will Disconnect the microcontroller and the DropBot may become Unresponsive. Do you still want to proceed? OK reboots; Exit does nothing.

Rarely: when the DropBot stops answering. Never in the middle of an experiment, since the connection drops.

Connection goes Inactive. MicroDrop then looks for the DropBot again. If it does not come back, unplug USB and power, and plug them back in.

Not needed in normal use.

Preferences

Open Edit ▸ Preferences. The DropBot settings are spread over a few pages.

Hardware Config

Read-only numbers the DropBot reports, and the last values it accepted.

Max Voltage (V) and Max Frequency (Hz): the DropBot's own limits, read when it connects. Last Applied Voltage (V) and Last Applied Frequency (Hz): the last values it accepted from the pane.

Before raising the pane's range, check the maximum here.

The maximums show inf until a DropBot has connected. The DropBot starts at the last applied values when it connects. Protocol steps do not change them.

Depends on the DropBot; last applied 100 V and 10 000 Hz on a fresh install.

Capacitance Update Interval

How often the DropBot sends a capacitance reading, in milliseconds.

Sent to the DropBot as soon as you change it.

Lower it for faster readings; raise it to reduce traffic.

The pane averages 5 readings, so at 100 ms its Capacitance can change at most every half second.

100 ms.

Drop Detect Capacitance

The capacitance above which a channel counts as wet in droplet detection.

Used by Find Liquid and by protocol features that check for droplets.

Raise it if dry electrodes are found as wet; lower it if small droplets are missed.

Compared with each channel's capacitance, measured at 1 kHz.

10 pF.

Protocol Setters Config

The range of the voltage and frequency boxes, in the pane and in the protocol tree.

Min Voltage (V), Max Voltage (V), Min Frequency (Hz), Max Frequency (Hz), and Default Voltage (V) / Default Frequency (Hz), which follow the last value set in the pane.

To stop anyone setting more than your chip can take, or to allow more if your DropBot can.

Open boxes take the new range at once. These are MicroDrop's limits only: keep the maximums at or below the Hardware Config maximums.

30 to 140 V, default 100 V; 100 to 10 000 Hz, default 10 000 Hz.

Realtime Mode Persistence

What happens to Realtime after a protocol run.

Prompt to keep Realtime Mode? and Keep Realtime Mode active?, plus Realtime Mode Pre-Protocol (s), the wait after switching Realtime on before the first step.

Untick the prompt once you always want the same answer.

Used only when Realtime was on before the run. A run started with Realtime off always switches it off again.

Prompt on, keep on.

Default Dielectric Material and Show Dielectric Info

The pane's two remembered choices.

Default Dielectric Material: the material picked when MicroDrop starts. Show Dielectric Info: whether the Dielectric row is shown.

Set once for the chips you use most.

Both follow the pane: picking a material or using the right-click menu changes them too.

Pick a dielectric...; shown.

Suppress no shorts information

Hides the No Shorts Detected window that appears on each connection.

Ticking Do not show again in that window sets it; untick it here to bring the window back.

When the window on every connection gets in the way.

Shorts Detected is always shown, and so is No shorts after a test you asked for.

Off.

Plate gap

The distance between the chip's top and bottom plates, which the droplet fills.

Volume = covered area × plate gap.

Set it for every chip design; a wrong gap scales every volume by the same factor.

From 1 to 2000 µm.

254 µm.

If you have an OpenDrop

With an OpenDrop, MicroDrop shows the Opendrop Status And Controls pane instead. It has the same Connection field, Realtime On / Realtime Off button and status-bar icons, with the same rules, except that there is no halt. It also shows the Board Id, three temperatures and the number of active feedback channels.

The OpenDrop sets its voltage and frequency on the board itself, so the pane only says Voltage: On-Board, Frequency: On-Board, Capacitance: N/A and Chip Status: See Instrument. Force, volume, dielectric, the self-tests and the dialogs on this page are DropBot-only.

Caveats

  • Switching Realtime on powers what is already lit. Clear the electrodes first if you do not want them driven.
  • A protocol run switches high voltage on even if Realtime was off, and the button is locked during the run. Treat high voltage as on during any run that is not a preview. In one recording the button read Realtime Off during a run; do not rely on its label then.
  • Find Liquid and three self-tests use high voltage with Realtime off.
  • After an over-current halt, check the chip before switching back on. The pane locks its Realtime button, but the status-bar icon and the T key check only the connection, not the halt.
  • The boxes are locked while a protocol runs, while a protocol step is selected, and after an over-current halt.
  • A value the DropBot refuses gives no warning. If the pane's maximum is above the DropBot's, the box shows the value but the measured voltage does not follow.
  • Calibrate for each chip and liquid. The calibration survives a restart; an old one gives confident but wrong force and volume.
  • Volume is an estimate and keeps its last value when Realtime goes off. If it reads 0.0 µL while Capacitance reads tens of pF with a droplet on the lit electrodes, reset and redo the calibration; if it stays at 0.0 µL, report it. This has been seen in a recording and is not explained yet.

Glossary

DropBot
The instrument that drives the chip's electrodes and measures capacitance.
Chip
The digital microfluidics device with the electrodes the droplets sit on.
Channel
One DropBot output; each electrode is wired to a channel.
Lit electrode
An electrode switched on in the Device Viewer (actuated).
High voltage
The DropBot's drive output, tens to over a hundred volts. Only on as listed in the Overview.
Filler
The medium around the droplets, usually air or oil.
Dielectric
The thin insulating layer between the electrodes and the droplets.
pF
Picofarad, a millionth of a millionth of a farad: the unit of the capacitance readings.
mN/m
Millinewtons per metre: force along each metre of droplet edge.
Halted
The DropBot cut the high voltage itself after too much current or too much load.
Short
A channel the DropBot finds connected where it should not be; MicroDrop offers to disable it.
Test board
A board from Sci-Bots that goes in place of a chip for the self-tests.
