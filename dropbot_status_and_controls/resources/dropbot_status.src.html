Dropbot Status & Controls

The Dropbot Status & Controls pane shows whether MicroDrop can talk to your DropBot, sets the voltage and frequency it drives the electrodes with, and reports what the board measures back.

Each card says what it is, when you would use it, how to read it, and the value MicroDrop starts with. The demo is live: drag the sliders.

Quick walkthrough

From plugging in the DropBot to moving a droplet with a known force. The Controls and icons section shows every icon as it looks in the app.

  1. 1Connect

    Plug in the DropBot's USB and power. MicroDrop finds it on its own: Connection turns to Active, and the picture and the status-bar droplet turn green once a chip is inserted (orange while there is none). Not found? Use Tools ▸ Dropbot ▸ Search for Dropbot Connection.

  2. 2Turn on Realtime

    Click Realtime Off so it reads Realtime On (or click the icon in the status bar). Now the DropBot applies settings and electrode changes as you make them, and the readings start to update.

  3. 3Set voltage and frequency

    Type or scroll in the boxes on the right. The left column is what the DropBot reports back. Values outside the allowed range snap to its ends; see Voltage.

  4. 4Calibrate, then read the force

    In the Device Viewer's calibration buttons, turn on a few electrodes under filler and click , then under liquid and click . The pane then shows c_device and the Force your voltage produces.

Settings and readings

Left column: what the DropBot reports. Right column: what you ask for.

Realtime mode

The switch between "change things on the hardware now" and "prepare settings without touching the hardware".

When on, every voltage, frequency and electrode change goes to the DropBot straight away and the readings update live. When off, changes are kept and only the latest voltage and frequency are sent when you switch it on again.

On while you work with droplets. Off to set things up first, or to make sure nothing is driven.

The button label and the green status-bar icon say it is on. While off, Voltage, Frequency, Capacitance and Force read "-".

Off. It can only be switched on while the DropBot is connected and not halted, and not while a protocol runs (the protocol controls the hardware then). A halt switches it off.

Voltage

How hard the DropBot drives the electrodes that are on: more voltage, more force on the droplet.

The right-hand box is the voltage you ask for (whole volts). The left-hand value is the voltage the board measures back, which can sit slightly off the request.

Raise it when droplets move sluggishly or not at all; lower it if droplets split, stick or the dielectric breaks down.

The box only accepts the range set under Preferences ▸ Protocol Settings ▸ Protocol Setters Config (Min/Max Voltage); anything outside snaps to its end. Edits are sent after you pause for a moment, so scrolling through values does not flood the hardware.

100 V, within 30 to 140 V. MicroDrop remembers the last value you applied.

Frequency

How fast the drive voltage alternates, in hertz (cycles per second).

The right-hand box sets it in steps of 100 Hz; the left-hand value shows it back while Realtime is on.

Most work stays at the default; change it when your chip or method calls for a different one.

The left-hand value repeats the setting (it is not measured), with an SI prefix: 10 kHz is 10000 Hz. Range and snapping work as for Voltage (Min/Max Frequency preferences).

10 kHz, within 100 Hz to 10 kHz.

Capacitance

What the DropBot measures across the electrodes that are on. Liquid over an electrode raises it.

The average of the last 5 readings, in picofarads; small wobbles are not shown, to keep it steady.

A jump when you turn on an electrode under a droplet means the droplet is there. "-" while Realtime is off.

Depends on the chip and how many electrodes are on: a few to hundreds of pF.

c_device

The chip's capacitance per square millimetre of electrode due to the liquid, from your calibration.

Liquid calibration minus filler calibration, in pF/mm² (see Calibration buttons).

Calibrate once per chip and liquid; it feeds Force and, if shown, Dielectric.

"-" until both calibrations are measured. Right-click the pane and tick Show Dielectric Info, then pick your dielectric material, to also see its thickness worked out from c_device.

No single value: it depends on the dielectric's material and thickness, and thinner layers read higher. The demo above uses 1 pF/mm² as an example.

Force

The pull on a droplet's edge per millimetre, from c_device and the measured voltage.

c_device × voltage² ÷ 2, in mN/m. Doubling the voltage gives four times the force.

To compare runs on different chips or voltages, or to set a voltage that gives the force a protocol needs.

Uses the voltage the board measures, so it follows the left column, not the box. "-" without a calibration or with Realtime off.

A worked example, not a target: 5 mN/m at 100 V with c_device = 1 pF/mm².

Caveats

  • Realtime off means nothing is sent. Changing voltage with Realtime off does not touch the hardware until you switch it on.
  • A halt switches Realtime off. After the halt message, check the chip and connections before switching it back on.
  • The boxes are locked while a protocol runs and after a halt; the protocol's own voltage and frequency apply then.
  • Force needs a fresh calibration for each chip and liquid; an old one gives a confident but wrong number.

Glossary

DropBot
The instrument that drives the chip's electrodes and measures capacitance.
Chip
The digital microfluidics device with the electrodes the droplets sit on.
Filler
The medium around the droplets, usually air or oil.
Dielectric
The thin insulating layer between the electrodes and the droplets.
pF
Picofarad, a trillionth of a farad: the unit of the capacitance readings.
mN/m
Millinewtons per metre: force along each metre of droplet edge.
Halted
The DropBot stopped driving after a fault, such as a short or an over-current.
