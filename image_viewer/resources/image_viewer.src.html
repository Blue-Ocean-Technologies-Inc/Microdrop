Image Viewer

The Image Viewer shows the pictures an experiment has captured, including the 16-bit raw frames of fluorescence captures that most picture programs show as almost black. It finds the images on its own, keeps up with new ones as they are saved, and lets you step through a series, set the contrast, zoom in and read the value of any pixel.

This tutorial covers finding and looking at images. Measuring them (regions of interest, the intensity plot, drift, AI detection, perspective correction) is the subject of the Image Analysis Tutorial, the next entry under Help ▸ Tutorials. The pictures on this page are sketches and made-up data, not screenshots.

Overview

The Image Viewer is a pane of the main window, like the Device Viewer and the protocol tree. It is not part of the first-launch layout, so you open it once from the View menu.

  • What it reads: image files on disk. By default it follows the current experiment's captures folder, where MicroDrop saves its captures, and opens on the newest image.
  • What it never does: change an image file. Contrast and zoom are display settings only.
  • The pane title names what you are looking at: Image Viewer - <experiment> - <file name> - 2048x1536 16-bit gray (size, bit depth and gray or RGB of the shown image). While a new image loads it reads Loading <file name>….

Quick walkthrough

From an open experiment to a well-displayed series you can step through, in six steps.

  1. 1Open the Image Viewer

    Open the View menu and click the entry that starts with Image Viewer. The entry carries the pane's title, so it may read Image Viewer - <experiment> - …. Click it again to hide the pane. You can drag the pane by its title bar to dock it elsewhere or float it.

    The pane opens on the current experiment's newest image. If the experiment has no captures yet the image area stays empty until the first one is saved.

  2. 2Pick the images you want

    In the sidebar, Image Groups holds two choices. Image Group is the kind of image (the subfolder it was saved in, such as 16bit_raw); Capture Session is one capture run. Then pick an image under Images, or drag its Seek slider.

    For another experiment, open Experiments and choose it there. brings you back to the current experiment.

  3. 3Make the image visible

    Auto contrast is on to start with, so a dark 16-bit frame already shows its droplets. Open Contrast and untick it when you want to set the black and white points yourself with Min and Max. Then every image is shown the same way and you can compare them by eye.

  4. 4Look closer

    Turn the mouse wheel over the image to zoom around the cursor, and drag to pan. fits the whole image again.

    Rest the cursor on a pixel: its position and stored value appear in the image's bottom-right corner (the pixel readout). Open Metadata for the file's details and any settings saved inside it.

  5. 5Step through the series

    and show the previous and next image; after a click on the image, the Left and Right keys do the same. plays them, one every 1.5 seconds, until you click .

    Stepping carries on into the next capture session when one ends. The counter at the right end of the button row (4/9) counts every image you can reach this way.

  6. 6Hand over to the analysis

    When the right images are listed, draw a region of interest (ROI) around a droplet with a tool on the right edge and click Calculate. MicroDrop measures the images the sidebar lists, nothing else.

    Carry on in Help ▸ Tutorials ▸ Image Analysis Tutorial.

Finding images

Images are found by where they sit on disk. Each experiment folder has a captures folder; inside it, a capture run may make a folder of its own (a capture session), and a session may sort its files into subfolders by kind (image groups). The viewer reads that layout back as four choices: experiment, image group, capture session, image.

Try it: the four choices

A made-up Experiments folder with three experiments. Change the choices, step with the buttons, and watch which files are listed (full colour), left out (faded) and shown (highlighted).

Experiment

Which experiment's images you browse. The section starts folded.

Experiment lists every folder in the Experiments folder (the folder the current experiment sits in), oldest first by when the folder last changed. Folders with no captures are listed too; choosing one just shows nothing. Choosing an experiment shows its first image, with Capture Session set to All.

Search narrows the list: type words, and only names containing every word stay (capitals do not matter). Experiment Seek drags through all experiments, oldest to newest, and the two arrow buttons step one at a time.

To look back at an earlier run, or compare two runs. New experiments join the list on their own.

The pane title shows the experiment folder's name. returns to the experiment MicroDrop is recording into.

The current experiment.

Image Group

The kind of image: the subfolder of a capture session that the files were saved in.

The list holds All (the start), then None for images saved straight into a session or into the captures folder, then each subfolder name found, for example 16bit_raw for the raw frames of a fluorescence capture.

Pick one kind before you step, compare or measure, so that every listed image is of the same kind.

All mixes the kinds. It merges every group of a session and orders the files by save time. When a capture saves a 16-bit raw frame and an 8-bit display copy of it, the two alternate as you step: the pane title flips between 16-bit gray and 8-bit gray, and with manual contrast the 8-bit copies look black. This is known and left as it is for now; choose the group you mean.

All when the pane opens; 16bit_raw for fluorescence measurements.

Capture Session

One capture run: a folder of its own inside captures.

A folder directly inside captures counts as a session when it holds subfolders (a fluorescence capture's session always holds its 16bit_raw folder). The list is All, then the sessions, oldest first by their first image. ungrouped holds the images outside any session folder, such as the Device Viewer's camera captures, which are saved straight into captures.

To look at one run. All lists every session of the image group in one go.

Session Seek drags through the list, oldest to newest; its first position is All.

The newest session when the pane opens; All after you choose an experiment.

Image, Seek and the counter

The images of the chosen session, and where you are among them.

Image lists the session's files by name, oldest first by save time. Seek drags through the same list. The counter n/N at the right end of the button row counts across every session of the image group, because the arrows carry on from one session into the next.

–/30 means the shown image is not among the listed ones. The next arrow click enters the list.

The newest image when the pane opens.

Filter (for example Wavelength)

A further choice that only appears when an installed plugin adds one.

A plugin can teach the viewer to read a value out of file names, for example the LED wavelength of a fluorescence capture. The row is then named after that value instead of Filter, and lists All plus every value found in the files. MicroDrop on its own adds none, so the row is hidden.

When one session holds captures taken with different light sources and you want one of them.

Changing the filter keeps the shown image if it passes, otherwise shows the first one that does. The counter and the arrows only reach images that pass.

All.

Following new captures

The viewer keeps its lists up to date while you work.

It looks for new files every 2 seconds. A picture the Device Viewer's camera has just saved appears at once. Other new files wait until they have not changed for 2 seconds, so a half-written file is never shown.

If you are on the newest image of the newest session (or on the last image with All sessions), the viewer moves to each new capture as it lands. Anywhere else, it stays where you are. While a protocol runs and a drawing tool, the scale ruler or Edit mode is on, it waits until you put the tool away.

Browsing any folder

opens any folder, inside an experiment or not.

The viewer searches the folder and every folder below it for .png, .tif, .tiff, .jpg, .jpeg and .bmp files, and sorts them into groups and sessions by the same rules.

Your ROIs, scale and other analysis settings are saved in an analysis folder: in the experiment folder when you browse an experiment's captures, otherwise in the folder you picked. A new folder therefore starts without them. goes back to following the current experiment.

Seeing an image

A camera frame holds far more brightness levels than a screen can show. The viewer picks which stored value is drawn black and which white; everything else is about looking closely.

16-bit and 8-bit images

How many brightness levels each pixel can store.

A 16-bit gray image stores values from 0 to 65535. Fluorescence cameras save their raw frames this way, and the signal usually uses only a small part of that range, so shown as it is the frame looks black. An 8-bit image stores 0 to 255 per channel; display copies, camera snapshots and most ordinary pictures are 8-bit.

The pane title ends with the size, the bit depth and gray or RGB, for example 2048x1536 16-bit gray. Contrast only applies to gray images: a colour (RGB) image is always shown as stored. A colour image with more than 8 bits per channel is shown at 8 bits per channel.

16bit_raw files: 16-bit gray. Device Viewer camera captures: 8-bit colour.

Auto contrast

Picks the black and white points for each image on its own.

The darkest 0.1 % of the image's pixels are drawn black and the brightest 0.1 % white, so a few very bright (hot) pixels do not wash out the rest. On a very large image the viewer reads an even sample of about a million pixels to find them.

Leave it on for browsing: every image shows its detail, whatever its bit depth.

Each image gets its own black and white points, so two images that look equally bright may not be. To compare brightness by eye, switch it off and use Min and Max. Display only: it never changes a measured number.

On. MicroDrop remembers the setting the next time it starts.

Min and Max

Your own black point (Min, the value drawn black) and white point (Max, the value drawn white).

Values at or below Min are black, at or above Max white, and the ones between are spread evenly over the grays. Both sliders run over the 16-bit range, 0 to 65535, and Min always stays below Max. They are greyed out while Auto contrast is on and keep your last values.

To compare images: the same window applies to every image, so a brighter droplet really looks brighter.

Raise Min until the background is just black; lower Max until the brightest droplet is just white. An 8-bit image holds nothing above 255, so with Max at thousands it looks black: that is the display, not the file.

Min 0, Max 10000 to start with; remembered the next time MicroDrop starts.

Zoom, pan, fit and Zoom Step

Moving around a large image.

The mouse wheel zooms around the point under the cursor; the zoom buttons and Ctrl++ / Ctrl+- zoom around the middle of the pane. Drag the image to pan (while no drawing tool, ruler or Edit mode is on). or Ctrl+0 fits the whole image. Zoom Step is how much one wheel notch or button click zooms.

Until you zoom, the image refits whenever the pane changes size. Once zoomed, stepping to another image of the same size keeps your zoom and position, so you can watch one droplet through a series; an image of a different size is fitted again.

Zoom Step 1.25 (allowed 1.05 to 2.0; 1.05 is barely visible, 2.0 doubles per notch).

Pixel readout

The position and value of the pixel under the cursor, for example (412, 98) = 3517.

The first two numbers are the column and row, counted from 0 at the top-left corner. After the = comes the value stored in the file: up to 65535 for a 16-bit image, not the gray you see on screen. A colour image shows three numbers, red, green and blue.

To check how bright a droplet or the background really is, or whether a spot is saturated (65535 on a 16-bit image, 255 on an 8-bit one).

With rolling-ball or perspective correction switched on (Image Analysis Tutorial), the image and the readout show the corrected frame, which is what gets measured. The readout disappears when the cursor leaves the image.

Metadata

Facts about the shown file and any details saved inside it. The section starts folded.

File Details first: Dimensions, Pixel Format (for example 16-bit grayscale), File Size and Modified (local time). Then whatever text the file carries. A PNG can hold named blocks of text; a block of settings gets a heading of its own and one line per setting. Rest the cursor on a name to see what it means, when the program that wrote the file says so.

What appears depends on what saved the image. Fluorescence captures (made by the fluorescence plugin, which is installed separately) carry a Capture Details block with their capture settings and the protocol step. MicroDrop's own camera captures carry none and show No embedded metadata. opens the same text in an Image metadata window that follows you as you step.

Tidying up experiments

Two ways to clear away experiment folders. Both move folders to the Recycle Bin (Trash on macOS and Linux), where you can restore them; neither ever deletes for good.

Send an experiment folder to the trash

Removes the experiment chosen in the Experiment list, with everything in its folder.

  1. Choose the experiment in the Experiment list, then click .
  2. MicroDrop asks Delete experiment '<name>' and everything in its folder? Answer yes to go ahead. Tick Don't ask again to skip this question in future.
  3. The folder goes to the Recycle Bin and the list moves to the next newer experiment (or the older one, if it was the newest).

The experiment MicroDrop is recording into cannot be removed: you get the message '<name>' is the current experiment and cannot be deleted while it is in use. To get the question back after ticking Don't ask again, open Preferences ▸ Image Viewer and, under Experiments, tick Ask for confirmation before deleting an experiment folder.

Prune Experiments

Clears away all the experiments that never captured anything.

It looks for experiment folders that hold only a logs folder (and perhaps empty folders), never the current experiment. MicroDrop asks Delete N experiment folder(s) that hold only logs?; the details list the folder names. When there are none it says No experiments to prune.

Afterwards it reports how many folders it moved. They drop off the Experiment list within a couple of seconds.

If MicroDrop offers an update first

Moving folders to the trash needs a small extra package, send2trash.

An installation set up before that package was added shows an Update required message instead: Sending experimental folders to the Trash needs the send2trash package, which this environment does not have. Update the pixi-microdrop repository to install it. It also says what the update resets in the pixi-microdrop folder and which of your installed plugins it adds back.

Update runs the update behind an Updating MicroDrop window, then carries on with the move, without a restart. Not now leaves everything as it was; nothing is deleted. If the update fails, MicroDrop shows the commands to run by hand.

On to the analysis

Just enough to get from a browsed series to the Image Analysis Tutorial, which explains every tool and number.

Which images get measured

Calculate (Ctrl+Return) measures exactly the images the sidebar lists.

That is the chosen Image Group, Capture Session and Filter, less any image marked as excluded. Set the choices first: usually 16bit_raw and the session you want, or All sessions for the whole experiment.

With the Image Group on All, raw frames and 8-bit copies are measured together, which mixes two brightness scales in one curve. While a protocol is running, Calculate asks first: A protocol is running; this can stall the app and delay captures. Continue?

Drawing a region of interest

An outline around a droplet that MicroDrop measures in every image of the series.

Pick a shape: ellipse (O), box (B), capsule (C) or contour (P), then drag on the image. The tool stays armed for the next one; Esc puts it away so dragging pans again. negative mask mode (M) makes the next shapes cut holes out of the ROIs they overlap instead, for a reflection or an electrode edge.

ROIs are saved with the experiment. To leave a bad frame out, press X or tick Exclude current image from analysis in Analysis Exclusion; you can still view it.

Keyboard

Keys for browsing

The viewer's keys work while the image has the keyboard, so click the image first.

Left / Right previous and next image; Shift+Left / Shift+Right previous and next experiment; Ctrl+0 fit; Ctrl++ (or Ctrl+=) and Ctrl+- zoom.

Press F1 (or Ctrl+/) on the image, or choose Help ▸ Image Viewer Keyboard Shortcuts…, for the full list, drawing keys included. Help ▸ Tutorials ▸ Shortcuts, Gamepad & Touch Assist Tutorial has a key finder covering every pane.

Caveats and limits

  • Image Group "All" mixes kinds. Raw 16-bit frames and their 8-bit copies alternate, and both are measured. Pick one group before comparing or calculating.
  • Auto contrast is per image. Do not judge brightness changes by eye with it on; use Min and Max, or measure.
  • Analysis settings follow the folder. Browsing a folder outside an experiment starts a fresh set of ROIs, scale and exclusions, saved in that folder.
  • The current experiment cannot be trashed from the viewer or by Prune Experiments.

Large series

The viewer loads and draws images in the background, so the window stays responsive.

Dragging a Seek slider never queues up every image you pass: the viewer loads the newest position next and skips the rest. The last 8 images loaded are kept in memory, so going back and forth over them is instant (a full 16-bit frame takes about 20 MB). Contrast changes redraw in the background too, so a dragged slider never freezes the window.

Folders are searched in the background. A folder is only searched again when something in it changed, and in any case every 30 seconds, because some drives do not report changes.

Not supported

Things you might expect from an image program that the viewer does not offer.

  • No false-colour maps (colormaps): gray images are shown in gray.
  • No histogram of the whole image in the viewer. The ROI Intensities pane has a Histogram view of the pixels inside your ROIs (Image Analysis Tutorial).
  • No saving or exporting of the shown image, and no copying it to the clipboard. Open its folder with to use the file elsewhere. The numbers and the plot export from the ROI Intensities pane.
  • No contrast for colour images, and no editing of pixels.
  • No videos: the viewer lists image files only.

Glossary

Experiments folder
The folder that holds one folder per experiment. MicroDrop creates a new experiment folder for each experiment and records into it.
captures
The folder inside an experiment where MicroDrop saves captured images.
Capture session
One capture run's own folder inside captures.
Image group
The subfolder inside a session that sorts images by kind, such as 16bit_raw.
16-bit / 8-bit
How many brightness levels a pixel can store: 65536 or 256.
Raw frame
The camera's image as measured, before any scaling for display.
Black point, white point
The stored values drawn as black and as white; together the display window.
Percentile
The value below which a given share of the pixels lie: the 0.1 percentile has 0.1 % of the pixels below it.
Hot pixel
A sensor pixel that reads far too bright in every image.
Saturated
At the top of the range (65535 or 255): the real brightness may have been higher.
ROI
Region of interest: an outline whose pixels MicroDrop measures.
Negative mask
A shape that removes pixels from the ROIs it overlaps.
Recycle Bin / Trash
Where removed folders wait until you restore them or empty it.
