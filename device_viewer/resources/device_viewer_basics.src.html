The Device Viewer

The Device Viewer is the map of your chip. You click electrodes here to switch them on, draw routes for droplets to follow, run those routes, and undo what you did not mean to do.

The short looping clips are recordings of MicroDrop; they play while they are on screen. A few marked Simulated are made from real footage and stand in until a recording replaces them. The chip pictures you can press ▶ Play or Step ▸ on are simulations that follow MicroDrop's own route rules: change the settings and watch what the app would do.

Where things are

The Device Viewer fills the left of the main window. Its side panel holds the settings, and the status bar underneath says where your clicks are going.

  1. The chip. Every electrode is outlined, over the camera picture when a camera is on. Electrodes that are switched on are filled blue.
  2. Reveal strip. Reveal Hidden Controls hides or shows the side panel.
  3. Side panel, in collapsible sections from the top: Viewport Controls, Camera Controls (with Alpha Settings inside), Paths, Zones and Calibration. Plugins can add more below.
  4. Paths: the route settings, the route table and the buttons that run routes.
  5. Mode buttons at the bottom of Paths: what a click or drag on the chip does.
  6. Status bar. Free Mode or Editing: Step 1: see Free Mode or a step?

Right-click the side panel (or the reveal strip) and choose Modify Layout... to set the side panel's width and the minimum heights of its tables (Device Viewer Sidebar Width, 320 px to start, and Alpha View Min Height, Layers View Min Height, Zones View Min Height).

Neighbouring topics have their own tutorials: loading a device file, channels and connections (#811), the camera and its alignment (#812) and zones (#813).

Quick walkthrough

From a droplet on the chip to a route that moves it, and back again.

  1. 1Switch electrodes on and off

    Click an electrode to switch it on (blue); click it again to switch it off. A droplet moves onto a switched-on electrode next to it, so switch on the one in front, then off the one behind.

    A click only changes the electrode's state. The DropBot puts voltage on it only while Realtime On is set (T) or a protocol runs.

  2. 2Draw a route

    Choose Draw (D) and drag from the droplet's electrode through the ones it should cross. Let go: the route is a new row in the Path table under Paths.

  3. 3Set it up and run it

    Open Execution Settings and set Duration, Length, Overlay and Reps. Leave on in the Run column for each route to run, then click Run selected routes. Tick Phases instead to step through it with .

  4. 4Undo a mistake

    Undo (Ctrl+Z) takes back the last change; Redo (Ctrl+Y) puts it back. To start over, Clear Routes (Shift+X) and Clear Electrode States (X).

  5. 5Zoom, pan and turn the view

    Use Viewport Controls, or Ctrl + mouse wheel to zoom and Space to drag the view around. R turns the chip a quarter turn; Shift+R turns the camera picture.

Clicking, the keyboard, the tooltip and the right-click menu.

Switching electrodes

In Draw or Edit mode a click (press and release on the same electrode) switches it on or off. Dragging draws a route instead; in Auto mode a click switches nothing.

Faster with the keyboard (click the chip first so it has the keyboard). Arrows follow the screen, however the chip is turned:

On a touchscreen, Tools ▸ Touch Assist ▸ Virtual D-pad puts the arrows on screen. Its ⇧, ctrl and alt stay on once tapped, until tapped again.

How much liquid is on the switched-on electrodes? The Volume readout in Dropbot Status, and its pop-out Droplet Readouts window, follow what you switch (see the Dropbot Status & Controls Tutorial).

The on/off state of each electrode. It is what a protocol step stores and what the DropBot applies.

Moving, holding, splitting or merging droplets by hand.

Blue fill: on. Red fill: disabled (cannot be switched).

All off when MicroDrop starts.

Electrode tooltip

Rest the pointer on an electrode to see what it is.

Three lines: Electrode ID: … (the name in the device file), Channel: … (the DropBot output it is wired to, the number routes and protocols use) and Area (mm²): … (after any area scaling). A disabled electrode adds [DISABLED].

Finding an electrode's channel; checking an area before you trust a volume.

On. Untick Enable Electrode Tooltip in the right-click menu to stop it.

Find Liquid

Asks the DropBot where there is liquid and switches those electrodes on.

A droplet-detection measurement over every electrode; the ones with liquid on them are added to the switched-on set.

After loading, or when you are not sure where the droplets are.

The menu item adds to what is already on; F switches everything off first, so only the liquid stays lit.

Needs a connected DropBot.

Disable All Electrodes

Takes every electrode out of use; Enable All Electrodes brings them back.

Disabled electrodes are filled red, cannot be switched on and say [DISABLED] in their tooltip.

Mostly one at a time, with Disable Electrode, for a damaged or shorted electrode. Disable all, then enable a few, to restrict work to a small area.

None disabled. Undo brings a disabling back.

Channel numbers

Routes, protocols and the tooltip name electrodes by channel, the DropBot output each one is wired to.

In Edit Electrode Channels mode (N; the label reads Mode: Channel Edit) a click on an electrode lets you type its channel, from 0 to Number of channels − 1; leave it empty to unassign it.

Only when the device file is wrong. Changing channels is covered with device files in #811.

Draw mode

Press on an electrode and drag through its neighbours to make a new route.

While you drag, the label reads Mode: Edit Draw; when you let go it is back to Draw. The new route is selected (drawn yellow).

Every new route. Drag back onto the starting electrode to close it into a loop.

Each neighbour you cross is one more electrode on the route. A droplet cannot jump, so stay on neighbours.

The mode MicroDrop starts in.

Edit mode

Change the selected route instead of making a new one.

Drag from any electrode of the selected route, or onto its start, to add to it; from the middle it adds an out-and-back detour. Hold Ctrl and drag with the right mouse button across the route to erase pieces of it.

A route that is nearly right. Select it first in the Path table.

In Draw or Edit mode, Alt + arrow grows the selected route one electrode that way and Backspace removes its last electrode.

Autoroute

Drag from the start electrode towards the destination: a pink preview shows the shortest path around your other routes.

Let go on the destination and the preview becomes a normal route, selected. Hold Alt as you press to let it cross existing routes. The mode stays Auto for the next one.

Long moves across the chip, and moves that must avoid other droplets' routes.

In this mode a click does not switch electrodes.

Pan mode

Drag the view around instead of the electrodes.

Toggle Pan/Drag (Space key) or Space: a left-drag scrolls the view and clicks on electrodes are ignored. Space again or Esc leaves it.

Zoomed in on a large chip.

The label reads Mode: Pan.

Merging routes

Join two routes into one, end to start.

Right-click a route ▸ Start Merge, then right-click the other ▸ Merge With. It works only when one route ends where the other starts. Stop Merging cancels.

Two pieces drawn separately that one droplet should follow in one go.

The Mode: label does not change while you merge.

Routes and the Path table

A route is a line across electrodes. When it runs, MicroDrop switches the electrodes along it on and off in turn, and the droplet rides along.

Draw a route, then run it

Simulated: the route is drawn from 22 to 79 and run with Length 2 and Overlay 1. Press ▶ Play or Step ▸.

The table names a route Path: 22 -> 79 (first and last channel), a loop Loop @ 40, and a route that goes round more than once 2x loop @ 40.

The selected route is yellow. Other routes take a shade of green; loops are orange or red depending on which way they turn. The tinted electrodes are the Route Shape: everything the run will switch on. The dark outline (Route Head) is where the selected route's droplet ends up.

Execution Settings

How the selected route runs. A run is a list of phases: one set of electrodes switched on for one Duration. Changes show at once in the Route Shape tint on the chip.

Try the settings

The panel on the left copies Execution Settings, with the app's limits. Pick a Try button or change any value; the chip replays the phases MicroDrop would switch.

Duration

Tooltip: Duration of each step in route (seconds).

How long each phase is held before the next.

Longer for viscous liquids or low voltage, when the droplet lags behind the electrodes.

Seconds, one decimal, 0 to 10000. A run takes about phases × Duration.

1.0 s

Length

Tooltip: Length of each step in route (# electrodes). Also called the trail length.

How many electrodes along the route are on at once: the droplet's footprint along its way.

Match it to the droplet: a droplet two electrodes long wants Length 2.

Bigger than the droplet only stretches it thinner; the liquid does not grow.

1

Overlay

Tooltip: electrodes actuated from one step to overlay onto next step. Also called the trail overlay.

How many of the Length electrodes stay on into the next phase, so each phase moves on Length − Overlay electrodes.

Overlay 1 with Length 2 makes a smooth crawl: the droplet always has an electrode it is already on.

At most Length − 1 (the box will not go higher). Where the footprint could not move without a gap, MicroDrop uses the smallest overlay that keeps it continuous.

0

Reps

Tooltip: Times to repeat loops execution.

Laps of a loop; at the end the droplet returns onto the loop's start. With Lin Reps it replays an open route instead.

Mixing: more laps, more mixing.

Greyed out and kept at 1 when no route is a loop and Lin Reps is off. Editing Reps sets Rep Duration back to 0.

1

Rep Duration

Tooltip: Seconds to repeat path executions. Idle time in end if loop cannot be completed.

Instead of a number of laps, a time: as many whole laps as fit (at least one), then the droplet waits at the start until the time is up.

When a step must last a set time, for example to mix for 30 s.

0 means off. Editing it sets Reps back to 1. Try Rep Duration 12 s in the simulator: the readout shows the laps and the wait.

0 s

Ramp Up

Tooltip: Ramp up overlay at start. A soft start.

The footprint comes on a row at a time from its tail until it is full, instead of all at once.

A droplet that tears or lags when a long footprint switches on at once.

Off

Ramp Dn

Tooltip: Ramp down overlay at end. A soft finish (clip under Ramp Up).

At the end the footprint goes off a row at a time from its tail, down to the head row.

To leave the droplet gathered on the last electrodes rather than spread over the whole footprint.

Off

Lin Reps

Tooltip: Replay linear paths Repetitions times.

Replays an open (not loop) route Reps times, each time starting again from its first electrode, not back and forth.

Rarely; the droplet must be brought back to the start in between by something else.

Off

Lanes

Tooltips: Extra lanes to the screen-left of travel (left slider) and …screen-right of travel (right).

Extra electrodes beside the route, making a wide footprint (a slug) for a big droplet.

Droplets wider than one electrode.

Left and right add up to at most Max slug width − 1 (4 to start). With Rot Lock off and In/Out on they mean inside / outside of the turns. Where a lane runs off the electrodes, the nearest free electrodes make up the count.

0 and 0

Rot Lock

Tooltip: Keep the slug's orientation through corners: it only translates.

On: the footprint keeps the orientation it set off with and only slides through corners. Off: it turns with the route, re-hung behind the leading electrode after every corner.

Off for long droplets that should follow a bend nose first.

A square footprint (Length equal to its width) always just slides. Ticking it swaps In/Out for Re-centre.

On

Re-centre

Tooltip: Centre a locked slug on the route when it moves across its own heading; off, it keeps the position it arrived in.

When a locked footprint moves sideways to itself, centre it on the route; off, the route runs along its edge.

On (shown only with Rot Lock on)

In/Out

Tooltip: Lanes read as inside / outside of the turn, not screen left / right.

The left slider counts lanes on the inside of each turn, the right one on the outside.

Loops and winding routes, where "left of travel" swaps sides at every turn.

On (shown only with Rot Lock off)

Apply to a step, and the Uncommitted prompt

With a protocol step selected, the panel shows that step's execution settings. Changing them changes the panel only.

Fixing mistakes

Undo and Redo

Undo (Ctrl+Z) and Redo (Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z).

Undoes route drawing and editing, switching electrodes on and off, disabling electrodes and alpha changes. It does not undo Execution Settings, mode changes, the eye or the Run column.

Greyed out when there is nothing to undo or redo, and while a protocol runs. Selecting a protocol step, or loading a device, starts the history afresh.

Start over

Clear Electrode States (X) switches everything off; Clear Routes (Shift+X) deletes every route. Both are also in the right-click menu, and both can be undone.

Free Mode or a step?

Where your clicks go depends on whether a protocol step is selected. The status bar says which.

Editing: Step 1: every electrode you switch and every route you draw is written into that step (and into every other selected step). Free Mode: nothing is selected, and your changes are kept aside. The next time you select a step, MicroDrop asks Unsaved Free Mode Changes: Insert as New Step keeps them as a new step, Step (free-mode capture); Discard Changes drops them.

Try things out in Free Mode; select a step when you mean to change the protocol.

While a protocol runs the bar reads Running Protocol: … and the chip is read-only (Mode: Display).

Realtime mode

Whether the DropBot follows the Device Viewer as you click.

With Realtime On and a DropBot connected, the electrodes you switch (and the phases you step or run) are put on the chip at once. Off, you are only editing the picture and the protocol.

On to move liquid by hand; off to draw a protocol without touching the chip.

Toggle with T, the button in Dropbot Status, or the status-bar icon. It is locked while a protocol runs.

Off

Caveats

  • A click is not voltage. Switching an electrode changes its state only; the DropBot powers it only with Realtime on or while a protocol runs.
  • Reps and Rep Duration are frozen (1 and 0) unless a route is a loop or Lin Reps is ticked.
  • Settings belong to the step. With a step selected, changing Execution Settings does not save them: click Apply execution params to step, or answer Commit when you move to another step.
  • Arrows move liquid, Alt+arrows draw. Arrow keys step the switched-on electrodes the way they point on screen, even with the chip turned. Turning is R (chip) and Shift+R (camera).
  • Execution Settings cannot be undone with Undo; set them back by hand.
  • Hidden is not deleted. A route with its eye off still runs if its Run cell is on.

Glossary

Electrode
One of the metal pads on the chip; switching it on pulls liquid onto it.
Channel
The DropBot output an electrode is wired to; routes and steps use it as the electrode's number.
Route (path)
A line across electrodes for a droplet to follow. One that returns to its start is a loop.
Phase
One moment of a run: the electrodes switched on together for one Duration.
Slug
A footprint more than one electrode wide, made with Lanes.
Route Shape
The tint over every electrode a route will switch on when it runs.
Step
One row of a protocol: electrodes, routes and settings.
Free Mode
No step selected: clicks are tried out, not written into the protocol.
Realtime
When on, the DropBot applies electrode changes as you make them.
Alpha
How opaque a layer is drawn, 0 to 100.
